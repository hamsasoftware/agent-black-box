# Summary

Implemented `plan_retention()` in `vaultsnap/retention.py`.

Which ADRs I applied: the overall model (set-aside snapshots, keep_last, independent bucket rules that skip empty periods, output order) is the first record. Its "Weeks" section is superseded, so weekly buckets are ISO weeks per the weeks record. the UTC record (UTC days) is superseded by the boundary record: every bucket key comes from `taken_at` converted to the offset, then shifted back by `day_start_hour`; `keep_last` and the future check use the real instant. the zones record replaces only the "no zone names" sentence of the boundary record: with `zone` set, the offset is looked up in the zone table at the real instant and `tz_offset_minutes` is ignored. the window record adds `keep_within_hours` (inclusive, real instants) as a sixth rule, but its section on the bucket rules was reverted by the revert record, so window snapshots are set aside (kept, not counted, never a representative). the chains record adds the dependency step: after all rules, ancestors of kept snapshots are held; held snapshots count toward nothing. the tags record gives `expire_tags` and the order of overrides (protect, future, expire, then the revert record's window, then the remaining snapshots), with the dependency step overriding expiry. the month record makes monthly buckets start on `month_start_day` with short months and leap years handled; yearly stays the calendar year. The the index index stops at the chains record and its status column is out of date, so I used the status line of each record instead. The README draft in the project root is older than the ADRs and was ignored.

Verified: `python -m unittest` (7 visible tests OK), plus a scratch script that checked the New Year weeks, a day start crossing a year, both zones around a transition, the window with keep_daily, a held parent that is newer than its child, expired snapshots, and month start day 31 around 29 February. Not verified: very large snapshot lists for speed.
